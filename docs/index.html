What it does

Captions for any audio

Set the speech language, and Textize picks that language out of whatever your Mac is playing and shows it as captions.

Everything on device

Speech recognition and translation both run on Apple's built-in frameworks. Textize does not send data anywhere.

Live translation

Choose original, original with translation, or translation only. You can also pick a single sentence to see its original or its translation.

Stays out of the way

The caption window does not block clicks or scrolling in other windows. It can hide itself after a period with no speech.

Scroll back

Scroll in the strip along the right edge to see earlier captions.

Six interface languages

English, 한국어, 日本語, Español, Français, Deutsch.

How it works

Textize takes the audio your Mac is playing through a Core Audio process tap and passes it to the speech recognition built into macOS. The recognized text goes into the caption window one sentence at a time. Processing uses only features built into macOS, with no data sent anywhere, and the only permission Textize asks for is System Audio Recording. Subtitles come from the translation feature built into macOS.

Speech models and translation language packs are downloaded the first time you use a language, and work without an internet connection. Speech models can be stored for up to five languages.

Requirements

  • macOS 26 or later
  • The System Audio Recording permission
  • A speech model for the language you want to caption
